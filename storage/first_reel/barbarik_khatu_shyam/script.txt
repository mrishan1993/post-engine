Before Kurukshetra began, a young warrior walked toward the battlefield. Barbarik, Bhima's grandson. He carried just three arrows — a gift from Shiva. The first marked every target. The second destroyed them all. His vow: he would always fight for the side that was losing. Krishna, disguised as a Brahmin, asked — with only three arrows? Barbarik shot one. It pierced every leaf on a peepal tree. It even circled Krishna's foot, because a leaf was hidden beneath. Krishna understood the danger. Whichever side Barbarik joined would start losing, so he would switch sides again and again, until only Barbarik remained. So Krishna asked for one gift: his head. Barbarik gave it without hesitation. His only wish was to watch the war. When it ended, the Pandavas asked who deserved the credit. Barbarik said: I saw only Krishna's Sudarshan Chakra cutting through everyone, and Draupadi's anger drinking the blood. Today he is worshipped as Khatu Shyam. Haare ka sahara. The support of the defeated.
